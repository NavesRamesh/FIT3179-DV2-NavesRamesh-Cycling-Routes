A DATA-LED EXPLORATION OF AUSTRALIA'S RECREATIONAL CYCLING LANDSCAPE

Australia's cycling routes take many forms. Some follow former railway corridors through regional landscapes, while others connect neighbourhoods and city spaces. This visualisation explores where selected trails are located, how their lengths and types vary, which age groups participate in cycling, and when cyclists use selected Brisbane bikeways.

06 — AGE-GROUP PARTICIPATION TRENDS

How has cycling participation changed across age groups?

Compare adult cycling participation rates across seven age groups over three consecutive AusPlay survey periods. Discover how participation patterns differ between generations.

Adults aged 55–64 and 65+ are the only groups with consistent increases across all three periods.

The 55–64 group rose from 11.73% to 12.10%, while the 65+ group increased from 5.53% to 5.77%. In contrast, participation declined across several younger and middle-aged groups.

Source: AusPlay By Sport Data Tables, 2023–24, 2024–25 and 2025–26. Figures represent estimated adult cycling participation generally, not specifically recreational trail use. The survey collection method changed in July 2023; this chart uses the post-change survey periods.

08 — CONCLUSION

So, what does Australia's cycling landscape tell us?

Australia's recreational cycling landscape is diverse, stretching from long-distance rail trails to shared urban pathways. The visualisations reveal differences in trail distribution and length across states, with Victoria recording the greatest open rail-trail length in the 2022 dataset. Individual trails also vary considerably, with the Brisbane Valley Rail Trail standing out as the longest trail in the selected dataset.

Cycling participation patterns show that adults aged 55–64 have the highest estimated participation, while older age groups are showing gradual increases over the periods examined. The Brisbane bikeway observations also highlight how cycling activity changes throughout the day and between weekdays and weekends, depending on the monitoring location.

Ultimately, cycling is not shaped by one factor alone. Trail availability, geographical differences, participation across generations and everyday travel patterns all contribute to Australia's cycling landscape. Understanding these patterns can help highlight where recreational cycling opportunities exist and where further attention may be needed.
